General

Sweety Pets is a European B2B pet inventory platform connecting professional suppliers with business buyers looking for available pet product stock.
No. Sweety Pets is designed for business-to-business transactions. The platform is intended for companies and professional buyers and suppliers rather than private consumers.
The platform focuses on pet products including available stock, overstock, slow-moving inventory, seasonal collections, discontinued products, clearance stock and other commercially usable pet product inventory.

Buying

Browse the catalogue, open an offer and review its commercial details. You can then submit a request for the quantity you need. The final quantity, price, payment and delivery conditions are confirmed before the transaction proceeds.
No. A request expresses your interest in a particular stock lot. The transaction is confirmed only after the relevant commercial conditions have been agreed.
Yes, when the supplier allows partial purchases. Your requested quantity must normally meet the minimum order quantity shown for the offer.

Selling

Create a supplier account, complete the required company information and create a stock lot containing product details, quantity, MOQ, price, warehouse location, available markets and other relevant information.
The platform is focused on available B2B inventory. This can include clearance products, overstock, slow-moving inventory, seasonal collections, discontinued ranges, previous packaging and other appropriate stock.
Supplier inventory information should be kept up to date. Available quantity, pricing and other commercial information should reflect the current status of the stock.

MOQ & pricing

MOQ means Minimum Order Quantity. It is the smallest quantity of a product or stock lot that the supplier is prepared to sell in a transaction.
The supplier determines the minimum order quantity when creating the product offer.
The offer provides the B2B price information supplied for the lot. The final commercial conditions should be confirmed before the transaction is completed.

VAT & payment

VAT treatment can depend on the supplier, buyer, countries involved and the nature of the transaction. The applicable VAT conditions should therefore be confirmed for each transaction.
Payment terms are confirmed as part of the commercial agreement for the transaction. The applicable payment process should be reviewed before the goods are released or dispatched.
Offers should clearly show the applicable currency. For European B2B transactions, EUR may commonly be used, but the actual currency is determined by the specific offer and transaction.

Delivery & logistics

Depending on the offer and agreed conditions, transport may be organised by the supplier, the buyer or agreed separately between the parties.
Not necessarily. Product price and transport cost should be reviewed separately unless the offer explicitly states that delivery is included.
The product offer should show the warehouse or stock location so that buyers can consider logistics before submitting a request.

Company verification

Company verification helps create a more professional B2B environment and makes it easier for users to identify the businesses involved in transactions.
Business information may include company name, registration details, business address, VAT information and contact details. The exact verification process may depend on the account and transaction requirements.

Returns & claims

B2B transactions do not automatically follow consumer return rules. Return conditions should be agreed between the parties and may depend on the terms of the specific transaction.
The buyer should document the issue and contact the relevant party promptly. Claims should be handled according to the commercial terms agreed for the transaction.

Sales markets

When listing stock, suppliers can specify the markets where their products are available for sale. An offer may be available across the EU or only in selected countries.
No. Suppliers can select the countries where they are prepared to sell their inventory.
Yes. Product distribution rights, labelling, regulatory requirements or other commercial restrictions may affect where particular products can be sold. Suppliers and buyers should confirm applicable requirements before completing a transaction.
